## Verdict

**`CompiledProblem` compiles to CasADi 3.8.0.** The Pyomo 6.10.1 / PyNumero / ASL route is rejected: it passes correctness and sparse composition, but fails the installability hard criterion as a pinned-dependency install, and on the measured comparison it is also the slower and the more expensive route to develop against; the tie-break clause is not reached. Neither route is blocked. CasADi is selected subject to the distribution terms of ADR 0006, which are real but bounded and do not touch the evaluation path.

## Context

The plan closed the candidate set at two routes and gave P03 a rule: "correctness, sparse composition, distributability, and installability are hard criteria; then compare measured runtime and development cost. Keep CasADi on a defensible tie. If a candidate is blocked, retain the exact build/error evidence and choose the demonstrated viable route. If neither works, stop model breadth, identify the smallest blocker, and propose a bounded ADR remedy. Do not spend another open-ended month comparing libraries." P02 built the two spikes through the frozen `CompiledProblem` boundary (`docs/interfaces-frozen.md` §1), matched them at a lifted 17 × 17 system with 60 nonzeros, and deliberately stated no preference. P03 audited both distributions byte for byte and reproduced both downloads from PyPI in clean environments. This ADR applies the rule to that evidence. It does not re-plan, does not survey a third backend, and does not touch the interface freeze: the boundary it selects a backend *for* is

```python
class CompiledProblem(Protocol):
    metadata: CompiledProblemMetadata
    def residual(self, x, context) -> EvaluationResult: ...
    def jacobian(self, x, context) -> JacobianResult: ...
    def reconstruct(self, x, context) -> ProcessState: ...
```

with the §2.1 semantics: sparse Jacobians in a documented CSC ordering; row and column IDs and source maps declared explicitly; `residual` and `jacobian` describe the same function at the same state; JVP/VJP/Hessian are negotiated capabilities; a missing exact Hessian is reported, never replaced by zeros. ADR 0001 (state `nTP-v1`, units, zero flow, signs) and ADR 0008 D1–D3 (no time at the boundary, `state_sha256` covers exactly `x`, per-row `accumulation`) are part of the same freeze and are not reopened.

## How the evidence is read

Three places where this verdict could go wrong were named in the brief. Each is ruled on here rather than left implicit.

**R1. The two routes do not assemble the same matrix, and are not timed at the same boundary.** CasADi eliminates the callback outputs and returns a Jacobian in the original variables; the grey-box route carries callback inputs and outputs as explicit primals with `gb.output_constraints[...]` rows, so the same toy problem is 2 × 2 on one route and 7 × 7 on the other, and the *inlined* 11 × 11 form exists only on CasADi. The only like-with-like comparison is the **lifted 17 × 17 / 60-nonzero form, which P02 built to be matched**, and every timing in D2 is of that form. The two "boundary" columns also do different work: CasADi's boundary names entries and assembles the canonical CSC; Pyomo's additionally applies a declared row-sign adapter to six grey-box rows and a name-keyed permutation into specification order. The ruling is that the **boundary level is the level the orchestrator pays**, so it is the level this ADR ranks on; the backend level is reported to show that the Jacobian gap originates in the backend and not in P02's bookkeeping (Pyomo's boundary adds 85 µs to a backend gap of 564 µs). Neither level is treated as "the" number, and the routes are never averaged across levels.

**R2. The Jacobian overhead ratio at n = 17 is not a scaling result.** The system has 17 variables and 60 nonzeros; a single point cannot separate a per-call floor from a per-nonzero slope, and this ADR does not claim to. What it does claim is stated in D2.2: the measured point is representative of the problem sizes the next two releases will actually run (SYN-001 is 21 rows; v0.0–v0.1 flowsheets are tens to low hundreds of variables), where per-call overhead multiplied by a damped Newton's evaluation count *is* the cost; and the verdict is constructed so that **it is unchanged if the ratio at scale turns out to be 1.0**, because the Pyomo route fails a hard criterion before runtime is weighed and the tie-break also defaults to CasADi.

**R3. METIS 4.0.** The CasADi wheel ships one object, `libcoinmetis.so.2.0.0` (306 208 bytes, three copies, 918 624 bytes of download), carrying METIS 4 code under a notice that forbids redistribution and restricts use outside non-profit and US-government settings to evaluation. Over-reading it would make it a blocker; under-reading it would note that the route never loads it and move on. The blueprint forbids the second ("disabling a bundled plugin does not itself establish that distributing its bytes meets this policy"), and the facts forbid the first: the project's default distribution mode ships none of the wheel's bytes, the object is not in the `DT_NEEDED` closure of `_casadi.so`, and the remedy for any vendored artifact is to remove three files. The ruling is that METIS **does not touch the backend criterion** (D1.3) and **does constrain what the project may vendor and which CasADi plugins may ever be on a default path** (ADR 0006 D2). It is neither nothing nor a blocker.

**Evidence classes.** The correctness checks are *numerical verification* against an independent expectation (the P01 references, computed separately at 20 digits and compared by a judge that itself is tested against a perturbed entry and a non-zero structural zero); they are not empirical validation of any thermodynamic model and not optimality evidence. The timings are *measurements* on one host under uncontrolled load (median of 200 calls after 20 warm-ups, minimum beside it) with no independent expectation; on their own they pin nothing and become a regression fixture. The audit's figures are graded *measured* / *declared* / *inferred* in `docs/p03-binary-audit.md` and are cited here with their grade. The status of every input is `tested` at most; nothing is `reviewed`.

## Decision

### D1. Hard criteria, in the plan's order

#### D1.1 Correctness — CasADi met; Pyomo met

The P02 manifest records 93 checks, 89 pass, 0 fail, 4 not applicable, and both per-backend verdicts `P02.verdict.casadi` and `P02.verdict.pyomo` are `PASS-composition`. (The prose header of `docs/p02-measurements.md` says 70 assertions; that count predates the Fable review that added assertions, and the manifest's 89/0/4 is authoritative.) The expectation is independent: `P02.A00.judge` first checks the harness's own `expected.py` against the P01 reference values, and the judge is shown to reject a perturbed Jacobian entry and a structural zero that is not exactly zero. Both routes describe the same function at the same state on the residual and Jacobian paths (the pairing identity of ADR 0008 D2 is asserted). Class: numerical verification on a synthetic subsystem. No criterion here separates the candidates.

#### D1.2 Sparse composition — CasADi met; Pyomo met

Both routes assemble the matched lifted system at 17 × 17 with 60 nonzeros and 792 bytes of stored structure, preserving the declared structural zeros. Per assembled Jacobian each route makes exactly one Jacobian call per block; the specification's threshold for suspecting a difference fallback is `n_inputs + 1` value calls per Jacobian (three for block K, six for block H) and both routes are far below it. Neither route densifies. Ordering is pinned by name on both, for different reasons (D5.1). Two capability facts are recorded but are not hard-criterion failures because the frozen interface makes them *negotiated*: forward and reverse products are **exact on CasADi and absent on Pyomo**; second order through opaque callback Jacobians is **absent on both**. The capability difference is weighed under development cost (D2.3), not here.

#### D1.3 Distributability — CasADi met (dependency mode), constrained for vendored bytes; Pyomo met (dependency mode), ASL unresolved for vendored bytes

The criterion is read as: *may the project distribute its product with this backend, in the distribution mode the architecture actually uses?* That mode is one installable package with pinned dependencies fetched from PyPI by the installer (blueprint §15), with replay bundles that reference dependencies by hash rather than embedding them (blueprint §15 "portable exports may omit restricted bytes and retain references"; K05).

*CasADi.* In that mode the project redistributes none of the wheel's 240 481 560 compiled bytes. What the P02 route loads is the `DT_NEEDED` closure of `_casadi.so`: `_casadi.so` (6 807 768 B) and `libcasadi.so.3.7` (12 576 848 B), 19 384 616 bytes, both CasADi's own LGPL-3.0-or-later code, plus system libraries. Nothing in that closure carries a restrictive notice, a GPL notice, or an unresolved notice. The restrictive object (METIS 4.0, `libcoinmetis.so*`) is reachable only through `libcoinmumps`, `libipopt`, `libsipopt`, `libuno` and, transitively, `libbonmin`, `libCbc`, `libCbcSolver`, `libCgl`, `libClp`, `libClpSolver`, `libOsiCbc`, `libOsiClp`, `libsleqp`, the `cbc` and `clp` executables, and seven plugins (`conic:cbc`, `conic:clp`, `linsol:mumps`, `nlpsol:bonmin`, `nlpsol:ipopt`, `nlpsol:sleqp`, `nlpsol:uno`; ADR 0006 D1.5, audit §2.6) — none on the route (verified from the installed objects' `DT_NEEDED` entries on 2026-09-17; see the note under "Acceptance evidence"). No proprietary vendor library is bundled; the twelve disabled objects (1 885 160 B) are shims and stubs. The criterion is therefore **met** for the dependency mode. For any artifact that would carry the wheel's bytes — a container image, an installer, a "batteries-included" bundle — distribution is **constrained** until ADR 0006 D2's remedy is applied and ADR 0006 D4's unresolved items are settled; that constraint is recorded, not waived, and it does not bear on the backend choice because the alternative is constrained in the same mode for a different reason (next paragraph).

*Pyomo.* The wheel is BSD-3-Clause with its notice in `dist-info/licenses/LICENSE.md`, one compiled object, no disabled bytes: cleaner notice hygiene than CasADi. But the route's essential component, PyNumero's ASL library, is built on the installing machine and the build leaves no notice for the AMPL Solver Library; its terms are **unresolved from the installed artifacts**. In the dependency mode the project ships no ASL bytes, so the criterion is **met** there too; in the vendored mode the ASL terms would have to be resolved first. On distributability the two routes are therefore at parity in the mode that matters and each carries one open item in the mode that does not yet exist. This criterion does not separate them.

#### D1.4 Installability — CasADi met on the measured platform; Pyomo not met

The criterion is read as: *can the backend the default install requires be obtained by `pip install` of pinned versions from PyPI on a machine with no compiler, on the platforms K05 names (Linux x86-64 and one of Linux aarch64 / macOS arm64)?*

*CasADi.* `pip install casadi==3.8.0` installs a manylinux wheel with no build step and imports immediately. The download was reproduced on 2026-09-17 into a fresh environment with `--no-cache-dir`: 2 376 of 2 384 files byte-identical, all 232 compiled objects, all 81 notices and all 1 681 headers reproducing exactly, zero shipped files differing, the eight differences being install-generated `__pycache__/*.pyc`. **Met on Linux x86-64.** The second K05 platform is unmeasured; that is a K05 obligation and a reversal trigger (Q1), not a defect of the evidence at hand.

*Pyomo.* After `pip install Pyomo==6.10.1`, `AmplInterface.available()` is `False`. After `pyomo download-extensions` it is still `False`. `pyomo build-extensions` fails outright without setuptools; with setuptools, cmake and C/C++ compilers present it builds pynumero, aslfunctions, ampl_function_demo, cspline_external and mcpp, fails APPSI for want of pybind11, and **exits 1 on its success path**; only then is `AmplInterface.available()` `True`. The ruling rests on the first fact — the route's required component is not obtainable from PyPI — and not on the nonzero exit, which the brief correctly refuses to promote to a blocker and which is weighed here only as friction: every developer machine and every CI runner needs a compiler toolchain and a build step whose exit status cannot be used as a health check. **Not met** as a pinned-dependency install. The route is **not blocked**: it was built, it ran, and its evidence is retained — this is a measured failure of a hard criterion, which is a different thing.

A reader who prefers a more generous reading of installability ("obtainable with a documented build") should note that the verdict does not depend on this reading: under it the routes tie on the hard criteria and D2 and D3 select CasADi anyway. The verdict has three independent legs — the installability criterion, the measured comparison, and the tie-break default — and the Pyomo route would have to prevail on all three to be selected.

### D2. Measured runtime and development cost — weighed only after D1

#### D2.1 Runtime, at the boundary and per Newton iteration

Host: Debian 13, x86-64, Python 3.13.5, single-threaded BLAS, NumPy 2.2.4 and SciPy 1.15.3 pinned identically in both environments; machine load not controlled; medians of 200 timed calls after 20 warm-ups, minima beside them. Values in microseconds, median / min.

| | CasADi backend | CasADi boundary | Pyomo backend | Pyomo boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Residual | 74.6 / 59.2 | 119.4 / 117.0 | 67.0 / 54.9 | 66.8 / 64.3 |
| Jacobian | 178.5 / 175.8 | 238.8 / 235.2 | 742.8 / 714.2 | 827.8 / 807.7 |
| **One residual + one Jacobian (a Newton iteration), medians** | 253.1 | **358.2** | 809.8 | **894.6** |

Read honestly: at the backend level the residuals are equivalent (the 7.6 µs difference is within the spread between median and minimum); at the boundary CasADi's residual costs 45 µs more than its backend call while Pyomo's boundary adds nothing, so **at the boundary the residual favours Pyomo by 1.8×** — the brief's and `docs/p02-measurements.md`'s sentence that the residual "costs the same at both levels" is not accurate at the boundary level and is reported as such. The Jacobian favours CasADi by 4.2× at the backend and 3.5× at the boundary. Per Newton iteration, the unit an orchestrator actually pays, CasADi is **2.5× cheaper at the boundary** (358 µs against 895 µs) and 3.2× at the backend. One-off costs point the same way and are not close: backend import 12.6 ms against 432.1 ms, compile of the lifted form 5.76 ms against 162.5 ms (the Pyomo figure includes writing the problem and loading ASL inside `PyomoNLPWithGreyBoxBlocks`), resident set after compile 48.2 MiB against 116.4 MiB. The inlined 11 × 11 form (2.29 ms compile) exists only on CasADi and is not compared.

#### D2.2 What the n = 17 point is and is not evidence of

A single problem size cannot distinguish a fixed per-call floor from a per-nonzero cost, so this ADR **does not rank the routes at scale** and no later document may cite D2.1 as if it did. Two things can be said. First, the Pyomo route's extra cost has visible per-call components — grey-box block dispatch through Python, the name-keyed permutation, and the extra primals the grey-box form carries — so the ratio is expected to *shrink* with n toward one set by the two compiled kernels, which is unmeasured; the expectation is stated as an expectation. Second, the measured point is a fair proxy for the problems the next two releases will run: SYN-001 has 21 rows, and the v0.0–v0.1 flowsheets (K02–T05) are tens to low hundreds of variables, where a damped Newton with bounded line search and phase-attempt restarts multiplies exactly this per-call cost by its evaluation count. The weight given to D2.1 is therefore "decisive for the release horizon in front of the project, silent about v0.2 and beyond", and the verdict is deliberately built not to need more than that (D1.4, last paragraph). The measurement that *would* speak to scale is named under "What would reverse this decision".

#### D2.3 Development cost — inferred from the adapters each harness needed, not measured

No development-cost measurement exists and none is invented. What P02 recorded is which adapters each route needed to satisfy the same frozen boundary. The Pyomo route needed: a declared row-sign adapter on six grey-box output rows (PyNumero writes `f(inputs) − output`); a name-keyed permutation of rows because `PyomoNLP` does not preserve constraint declaration order (`e2` returned as row 0 for a model declaring `e1`, `e2`); explicit primals for every callback input and output, so no inlined form and a larger system for the same flowsheet; one Jacobian probe per block at compile time; a compiler toolchain on every machine; and it offers no JVP/VJP, which K03's directional-derivative checks and M03's sensitivities would otherwise have to build. The CasADi route needed: a sparsity declaration on every callback (`has_jac_sparsity` / `get_jac_sparsity`, else the pattern is over-approximated) and care to fill sparse `DM` values in column-major order rather than triplet order — both one-time obligations that K01 discharges with a conformance fixture — and it provides exact JVP/VJP without further work. On this evidence the CasADi route is the cheaper one to build on. This is a judgment from recorded facts, labelled as such.

### D3. The tie-break clause is not reached

"Keep CasADi on a defensible tie" applies when the hard criteria and the measured comparison do not separate the candidates. They do: the Pyomo route fails installability (D1.4) and, independently, loses the measured comparison (D2). The clause is recorded as **not reached**. Had it been reached — had installability been read generously and the runtime ratio been within the median-to-minimum spread — the tie would have been defensible on the one-off costs alone (import 34× and compile 28× cheaper, resident set 2.4× smaller), and the clause would have selected CasADi. There is no reading of this evidence under which the Pyomo route is selected.

### D4. The selected backend and its terms

1. **Backend.** `CompiledProblem` compiles to CasADi, pinned at `casadi==3.8.0` (the audited and reproduced wheel), through the P02 composition mechanism: a `casadi.Callback` subclass per opaque block implementing `get_sparsity_in/out`, `has_jacobian`, `get_jacobian` and the sparsity declaration of D5.2, embedded in an `MX` graph, with the coupled Jacobian assembled by `casadi.jacobian` and returned through the frozen boundary in the documented CSC ordering.
2. **Linear solve.** Stays in SciPy SuperLU with explicit options (D03, ADR 0004). No default path loads a CasADi `Linsol` or `nlpsol` plugin; after ADR 0006 D2 this is a distribution constraint as well as a numerical policy, because `linsol:mumps`, `nlpsol:ipopt` and everything else in the METIS closure would put a use-restricted component on the default path.
3. **Pin discipline.** The pin and the wheel's per-file hashes are the [A10] baseline. Changing the pin refreshes the audit (blueprint §15 [A10], T08) before the new pin is merged; K05's dependency-mismatch detection treats the backend version as structural.
4. **Rejected route retained.** The Pyomo/PyNumero/ASL harness, its results and its build record stay in the repository as evidence, not as a maintained backend. It is not a fallback: reviving it takes a new ADR under "What would reverse this decision".

### D5. What K01 inherits

These are obligations the evidence established; K01 discharges each with a conformance fixture and reports any it cannot discharge in `docs/progress.md` as a blocker, never by omission.

1. **Ordering is pinned by name, never by position.** CasADi fills a sparse `DM` in column-major (CCS) order, not triplet declaration order; values supplied in triplet order silently produce a permuted Jacobian. The production compiler maps every nonzero by `(row_id, column_id)` into the documented CSC ordering of `CompiledProblemMetadata`, and a fixture asserts the assembled pattern *and values* against a declared expectation at more than one state (the P02 judge is the model: it caught a perturbed entry and a non-zero structural zero).
2. **Every callback declares its Jacobian sparsity.** A `casadi.Callback` without `has_jac_sparsity` / `get_jac_sparsity` yields an over-approximated assembled pattern — structurally wrong, not merely slow. The property-provider plumbing (K02) must derive the declaration from the provider's capability contract, and a fixture must show that an over-declared and an under-declared block are both detected. Whether a *real* provider's declared sparsity is correct is untested by P02 and is K02's to test.
3. **JVP/VJP are available and exact on this route** and are negotiated capabilities under the frozen interface. K01 exposes them as capabilities with their own fixtures against a finite-difference oracle; nothing may assume them without the capability check.
4. **Second order.** Through an opaque callback Jacobian, the exact Hessian is **absent** and K01 reports it as unsupported — never as zeros (frozen interface, §2.1). When a block ships symbolic derivative code, CasADi returns exact second derivatives; K01 records that as a per-block capability, with a fixture, and M03's Hessian policy is written against the negotiated capability rather than against an assumption.
5. **Callback call accounting.** CasADi makes one value call per block per assembled Jacobian in addition to the Jacobian call, because its Jacobian callback takes the block's nominal output. K01 preserves the P02 counters so that a regression toward `n_inputs + 1` value calls per Jacobian — the differencing signature — fails a test.
6. **From ADR 0008 D4:** `constants_sha256` over the full pinned-input vector with a new `parameter_ids` field; an inert-`workspace` test; `row_accumulation` in `CompiledProblemMetadata` at schema promotion; and the frozen field set of `EvaluationContext` and arities of the three methods. `state_sha256` covers exactly `x` (ADR 0008 D2); the backend adds no coordinate.
7. **No backend import in the orchestrator** (plan §4.2 row K01). The `casadi` import lives in the compiler and the adapter only.
8. **From ADR 0006:** the dependency is declared as a pinned requirement with the third-party notice statement (ADR 0006 D5.1); K01 does not vendor, mirror or copy any wheel bytes into the repository or into a bundle.

## Alternatives considered

- **Select the Pyomo/PyNumero/ASL route.** Rejected: fails installability as a pinned dependency (D1.4); 2.5× slower per Newton iteration at the boundary and 34× / 28× slower to import / compile at n = 17 (D2.1); no JVP/VJP; no inlined form; needs a row-sign adapter and a name permutation the other route does not (D2.3). Its genuine advantages — BSD-3-Clause with a shipped notice, one compiled object, zero disabled distributed bytes, no restrictive notice — are advantages in the *vendored* distribution mode, where it carries an unresolved ASL notice of its own, and they do not outweigh a failed hard criterion.
- **Select neither and raise a blocker.** Rejected: no hard criterion is `blocked` or `insufficient evidence` on the platform measured. The only evidentiary gap is the second K05 platform, which is K05's deliverable and is recorded as a reversal trigger (Q1), not as a reason to withhold a verdict K01 needs to start.
- **Treat METIS 4.0 as failing CasADi's distributability.** Rejected: the project's default distribution mode ships none of the bytes; the object is outside the route's `DT_NEEDED` closure; the vendored-mode remedy is bounded (ADR 0006 D2). Over-reading it would select a route that fails installability instead.
- **Treat the `build-extensions` nonzero exit as blocking Pyomo.** Rejected, as the brief directs: APPSI is not on the route, the needed libraries built, `AmplInterface.available()` became `True`. Weighed as friction under D1.4 only.
- **Rank on a single headline timing, or compare CasADi's inlined form against Pyomo.** Rejected in P02 and here: the routes are matched only on the lifted form and only with both levels reported (R1).
- **Extrapolate the 4× Jacobian ratio to production scale.** Rejected (R2, D2.2): one point is not a slope. The verdict is built so it does not need the extrapolation.
- **A survey of further backends** (JAX, symengine, ADOL-C, hand-written AD). Out of scope by the plan's last sentence; the candidate set was closed before P02 and is not reopened.

## Consequences

- K01 turns the CasADi spike (`spikes/p02/casadi/`) into the production compiler and callable adapter, discharging D5.1–D5.8 with fixtures.
- `pyproject.toml` gains `casadi==3.8.0` as a pinned runtime dependency (Opus session, at K01), together with the notice statement ADR 0006 D5.1 requires; no `LICENSE` file is added by this ADR.
- The Pyomo environment (`.venv-pyomo`, `scripts/build-backend-envs.sh`) is kept only as long as the P02 evidence must remain reproducible; it is not a supported configuration.
- D03's explicit SuperLU choice becomes load-bearing for D04 as well (D4.2).
- The requirements ledger: this ADR supplies the "selection ADR" element of D02's minimum evidence and closes A05's comparator; the Opus session records the evidence pointer and the honest status (recommended in the P03 report; the session owns the manifest).

## What this ADR does not establish

- It selects a backend on a **synthetic 17-variable composition test**; it is not a solver result, not a performance benchmark on a real flowsheet, and not a scaling result (D2.2).
- It is **not empirical validation** of any thermodynamic model; SYN-001 is synthetic.
- It does not establish that a **real property provider's** declared sparsity is correct, or that the callback pattern is adequate for a provider that is not P02's — K02 and M01 establish that.
- It does not establish **second-order capability** through the callback boundary; it records its absence on both routes.
- It establishes installability on **one host and one platform**; no CI has executed any of it, and the second K05 platform is unmeasured.
- It does not establish that the project **may vendor** the CasADi wheel's bytes (ADR 0006 D2, D4), nor anything about the project's own rights (ADR 0006 D6).
- It carries **no human numerical or process-modeling review**; `review.numerical` and `review.process_model` are `pending` on every package.
- It does not change the frozen interface, any tolerance, any test inventory, or any requirement's status; it does not relax any gate.

## What would reverse this decision

Reversal takes a new Fable-authored ADR recorded in the decision register; none of the following may be acted on silently.

- **T1 — a composition failure on a real provider.** A K02 or M01 property provider whose composition through `casadi.Callback` fails a conformance fixture (wrong assembled pattern or values at a registered state) that the same provider passes through the grey-box route, with both runs retained.
- **T2 — a measured scaling result.** On a registered T06 flowsheet with at least 10³ variables, at the *same boundary* and on the *same host*, the CasADi route's cost per Newton iteration exceeding an alternative's by a factor that changes the solve budget — with the alternative's installability and distributability re-established first. A ratio measured at a different boundary, or on a different machine, does not qualify.
- **T3 — a change in what CasADi ships or where.** A pin change whose refreshed [A10] inventory puts a restrictive, GPL, or unresolved-notice object into the `DT_NEEDED` closure of `_casadi.so`; or no wheel for a K05 platform.
- **T4 — a rights determination.** As written, T4 had two grounds. **The first is spent:** Frank answered ADR 0006 Q1 on 2026-09-17 and allowed the LGPL, so a ruling that CasADi's LGPL-3.0-or-later dynamic use is excluded by blueprint §15's "no GPL-licensed components" line can no longer fire this trigger. **The second stands:** a ruling that the METIS bytes are unacceptable even in dependency mode (ADR 0006 Q2, open with its default applied), or a later rights determination on a ground not yet raised. Under T4, the Pyomo route's installability failure stands, so the honest outcome is "neither works": stop model breadth, and the smallest blocker becomes obtaining an ASL notice and a distributable PyNumero build, or a CasADi wheel built without METIS 4 — not a survey.
- **T5 — a second-order requirement** that M03's Hessian policy cannot meet through the callback boundary on CasADi and demonstrably can on the alternative. Today both routes are equally absent, so this cannot trigger without new evidence on both.
