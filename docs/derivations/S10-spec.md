## 2. Notation

- **Components** are i, j, k = 1…N, in the records' order. That order is the identity order of every n-vector.
- **State:** `nTP-v1`, with n in mol/s, T in K and P in Pa. Write n_tot = Σ n_i, and x = n/n_tot for a phase (a
  liquid's composition), y for a vapour's and z for a feed's.
- θ = T / (1000 K) and θ₀ = 0.29815.
- R = 8.31446261815324 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, exact. T₀ = 298.15 K and P° = 10⁵ Pa.
- "Flowing" means n_i > 0. F is the set of flowing components of a feed.

## 3. The model: closed forms, derived from one potential

### 3.1 The ideal gas and the datum

**(3.1)** c_p,i^ig(T) / R = b_i0 + b_i1 θ + b_i2 θ² + b_i3 θ³ + b_i4 θ⁴

**(3.2)** h_i^ig(T) = Δ_fH_i + R·(1000 K)·Σ_{k=0}^{4} b_ik (θ^{k+1} − θ₀^{k+1}) / (k+1)

So h_i^ig(T₀) = Δ_fH_i exactly (claim PURE-*-1).

**For the real records,**
- b_ik = a_{i,k+1}·1000^k, computed exactly in decimal from the NASA-7 low-range coefficients a₁…a₅ (M01's scaling,
  ADR 0026 D5);
- Δ_fH_i = R·(a₁T₀ + a₂T₀²/2 + a₃T₀³/3 + a₄T₀⁴/4 + a₅T₀⁵/5 + a₆), the polynomial's own H(298.15 K). By NASA's
  convention this is the standard formation enthalpy, with the elements at zero.

This is the formation datum of the conventions `WILSON-IG-ref-v1` (real) and `S10-VER-ref-v1` (verification;
invented Δ_fH). For both conventions:
- Neither is added to ADR 0011 D2's reaction-consistent set (R-704).
- Streams never mix with another convention (ADR 0001 D5.2).

### 3.2 The vapour pressure

**(3.3)** ln(P_i^sat / Pa) = a_i + b_i/θ + c_i ln θ + d_i θ²

**(3.4)** d ln P_i^sat/dT = (−b_i/θ² + c_i/θ + 2 d_i θ) / (1000 K)

d² ln P_i^sat/dT² = (2 b_i/θ³ − c_i/θ² + 2 d_i) / (1000 K)²

The c ln θ term is the constant-Δc_p (Kirchhoff) term: with only (a, b, c), Clapeyron with an ideal-gas vapour gives
Δc_p = R c exactly. The d θ² term carries the residual curvature. This is DIPPR eq. 101 with exponent E = 2, written in
θ so that every coefficient is dimensionless.

### 3.3 The pure liquid: one potential

The pure liquid's chemical potential is referred to the ideal gas, with the liquid's fugacity equal to the vapour
pressure: no Poynting factor, and φ^sat = 1 because the vapour is ideal.

μ_i^0L(T, P) = μ_i^ig(T, P°) + R T ln(P_i^sat(T) / P°)

It is independent of P, so the liquid's molar volume implied by the potential is zero (§3.7). The Gibbs–Helmholtz
relation h = −T² ∂(μ/T)/∂T, applied to the difference μ_i^0L − μ_i^ig(P°) = R T ln(P_i^sat/P°), gives:

**(3.5)** Δh_i(T) ≡ h_i^ig(T) − h_i^0L(T) = R T² d ln P_i^sat/dT = R·(1000 K)·(−b_i + c_i θ + 2 d_i θ³)

**(3.6)** h_i^0L(T) = h_i^ig(T) − Δh_i(T)

c_p,i^0L(T) = c_p,i^ig(T) − R (c_i + 6 d_i θ²)

Δh_i is the enthalpy of vaporisation implied by Clapeyron with an ideal-gas vapour and no liquid volume. **No separate
ΔH_vap or liquid c_p is used.** This is how blueprint §6.2's consistency requirement is met, and claims CONS-VA, CONS-VB
and CONS-VC check (3.5) against a 50-digit derivative of (3.3). The cost is accuracy: Δh_i is high by about 1/Z^V_sat,
and c_p^0L inherits the slope of that error. VAL-3 (§9.6) measures both.

### 3.4 The Wilson liquid

Λ_ij > 0 are constants, with Λ_ii = 1. Define S_i(x) = Σ_j x_j Λ_ij. Then:

**(3.7)** g^E / (R T) = −Σ_i x_i ln S_i

**(3.8)** ln γ_i = 1 − ln S_i − Σ_k x_k Λ_ki / S_k

(3.8) is ∂(n g^E/RT)/∂n_i, and claim ID-GE checks Σ x_i ln γ_i = g^E/RT. Differentiating with x treated as free
variables gives:

**(3.9)** G_ij ≡ ∂ ln γ_i/∂x_j = −Λ_ij/S_i − Λ_ji/S_j + Σ_k x_k Λ_ki Λ_kj / S_k²

Two identities follow, both used below:
- **G is symmetric** (by inspection; claim ID-GSYM).
- **Row sums are −1:** Σ_k x_k G_ik = −Σ_k x_k Λ_ik/S_i − Σ_k x_k Λ_ki/S_k + Σ_m x_m Λ_mi (Σ_k x_k Λ_mk)/S_m² = −1
  (claim ID-ROW).

g^E/RT depends on neither T nor P. Hence h^E = −R T² ∂(g^E/RT)/∂T = 0 and v^E = 0, exactly. **This is a consequence of
constant Λ, not an extra approximation.** It is stated as a limitation in §3.7.

### 3.5 What `evaluate_phase` returns

**(3.10)**
- Liquid: ln φ_i^L(T, P, x) = ln γ_i(x) + ln P_i^sat(T) − ln P
- Vapour: ln φ_i^V = 0

So ln f_i = ln(x_i P) + ln φ_i in both phases, and K_i = φ_i^L/φ_i^V = γ_i P_i^sat/P. A consumer written for φ–φ
providers (`pr-c1-v1`) reads this method unchanged.

**(3.11)**
- Liquid: h^L(T, x) = Σ_i x_i h_i^0L(T)
- Vapour: h^V(T, y) = Σ_i y_i h_i^ig(T)

h is the phase's molar enthalpy in J/mol, as for `pr-c1-v1`. The mixture form of the consistency is
h^L − Σ x_i h_i^ig = −T² ∂/∂T [R Σ x_i ln(x_i γ_i P_i^sat/P°)] (claim CONS-MIX).

### 3.6 Derivatives (order 1) and their identities

The n-derivatives of an intensive X(T, P, x) are the derivatives along the simplex (the projection of `pr-c1-v1`, M01
spec §4.6):

∂X/∂n_j = (∂X/∂x_j − Σ_k x_k ∂X/∂x_k) / n_tot

This is independent of how X is extended off the simplex.

| Property | ∂/∂T | ∂/∂P | ∂/∂n_j |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ln φ_i^L | d ln P_i^sat/dT (3.4) | −1/P | (G_ij − Σ_k x_k G_ik)/n_tot = (G_ij + 1)/n_tot |
| ln φ_i^V | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| h^L | Σ_i x_i c_p,i^0L | 0.0 | (h_j^0L − h^L)/n_tot |
| h^V | Σ_i y_i c_p,i^ig | 0.0 | (h_j^ig − h^V)/n_tot |

**Identity zeros.** Every 0.0 in the table is an identity of the model. The provider returns it as the answer `0.0`,
present in the result and not omitted (S10.A06; claim ZERO-ALL counts 102 such entries at the E-states).

**Identities that hold on any correct implementation** (claims at 50 digits, S10.A08 on the implementation):
- Gibbs–Duhem: Σ_i x_i ∂ln φ_i^L/∂n_j = 0. It follows from the symmetry of G and its row sums of −1.
- Symmetry: ∂ln φ_i^L/∂n_j = ∂ln φ_j^L/∂n_i.
- Homogeneity: Σ_j n_j ∂X/∂n_j = 0 for every X.

**Structural zeros at a pure state** (E3, registered because they vanish there):
- ∂ln φ_c^L/∂n_j = 0 for the flowing component c (Gibbs–Duhem with x_c = 1);
- ∂X/∂n_c = 0 for every X (homogeneity with one flowing component).

Claim VAN-02 checks that they vanish at E3 and do not vanish at E1.

### 3.7 Approximations this model makes, with their effect

| Approximation | Effect on the supported calculations | Where measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ideal-gas vapour | K_i loses φ_i^V and φ_i^sat. The fitted Λ absorbs part of this at the fit's pressure. Δh_i is high by roughly 1/Z^V_sat, which matters for energy balances. | VAL-1/2 (K), VAL-3 (Δh, c_p^L) |
| No Poynting factor; liquid volume zero in the potential | K_i is low by a factor of at most exp(v_i^L P/(RT)). With v_i^L ≤ 6 × 10⁻⁵ m³/mol (ethanol ≈ 5.8 × 10⁻⁵), this is ≤ v_max P_max/(R T_min) = 6e-5 × 2e5/(8.314 × 300) = **4.8 × 10⁻³** on the real domain. The liquid `v` is not offered. | arithmetic above |
| Constant Λ, so h^E = 0 | The real excess enthalpy of alcohol–water mixtures (negative, of order 10² to 10³ J/mol) is absent. γ is extrapolated in T away from the fit's isobar. | VAL-2 (T-extrapolation) |
| One liquid | No LLE. Exact within the model (§4.1). Not a statement about the real mixture. | n/a |

## 4. Stability: why two tests are a global analysis within the model

### 4.1 Theorem: the Wilson liquid is strictly convex in composition

Write the liquid's molar Gibbs energy of mixing as f(x) = Σ_i x_i ln x_i + Σ_i x_i ln γ_i. By (3.7):

f(x) = Σ_i x_i ln(x_i / (Λx)_i)

**Convexity.** Let φ(u, v) = u ln(u/v) for u, v > 0. It is the perspective of −ln, so it is jointly convex. Its Hessian
is (1/u)·w wᵀ with w = (1, −u/v)ᵀ, which is positive semi-definite. Hence F(x) = Σ_i φ(x_i, (Λx)_i) is convex on the
positive orthant: it is a sum of jointly convex functions of the linear map x ↦ (x, Λx).

**Strictness.** The second variation along d is:

d²F = Σ_i (1/x_i) (d_i − x_i (Λd)_i/(Λx)_i)²

It vanishes only if d_i/x_i = (Λd)_i/(Λx)_i ≡ r_i for every i, that is, Σ_j Λ_ij x_j (r_j − r_i) = 0 for every i. Take
i* = argmax r. Every term Λ_i*j x_j (r_j − r_i*) is ≤ 0, because Λ > 0 and x > 0. So all r_j are equal, and d = r·x.
On the simplex Σ d = 0, which forces r = 0 and d = 0.

So f is strictly convex on the open simplex, and on every face, by the same argument over the flowing components only.
∎

The ideal-gas vapour's f^V(y) = Σ y_i ln y_i (plus terms linear in y) is strictly convex too.

The Hessian of n·f with respect to n at n_tot = 1 is M = diag(1/x) + G. Since Gx = −1, Mx = 0. Claim CVX-01 shows M is
positive definite on Σd = 0 over a grid, for the verification Λ: minimum eigenvalue 1.94. That is an illustration; the
theorem covers every Λ > 0.

### 4.2 Consequences

1. **At most one liquid and one vapour.** Two coexisting phases of one model would need a tangent plane touching a
   strictly convex surface at two distinct points.
2. **One stationary point per trial.** For a trial phase of model π against a tangent plane with chemical potentials d,
   the distance D(w) = Σ w_i (ln w_i + ln φ_i^π(w) − d_i) is strictly convex. So it has at most one stationary point in
   the relative interior, and that point is its global minimum.
   - Flowing components are pushed into the interior (∂D/∂w_i → −∞ as w_i → 0).
   - Absent components (d_i = −∞) have w_i = 0.
   - Michelsen's unnormalised form tm(W) = 1 + Σ W_i (ln W_i + ln φ_i(W/ΣW) − d_i − 1) has the same stationary points.
     There tm* = 1 − ΣW*, with the sign of D*.
   - Claim CVX-02 shows the liquid-trial search at F2 and F10 reaching one point from five starts.
3. **A converged L–V tangent is the global minimum.** The common tangent plane touches the convex liquid surface at x
   and the convex vapour surface at y. Each surface lies above its own tangent plane.
4. **No trivial solution.** The phases use different models. A liquid trial against a vapour feed z is stationary where
   W_i K_i(w) = z_i. w = z would need every K_i(z) to be equal, which happens only at an azeotrope at its boiling point.
   That is the physical degenerate state (F12), not the spurious root that stopped IDAES's PR route (ADR 0026).
5. **A pure component** needs no special case:
   - tm_V = 1 − P^sat/P and tm_L = 1 − P/P^sat;
   - both are ≥ −ε_tpd only within the resolution band, where step 2 keeps the liquid (claim PURE-01).

### 4.3 The labels, operationally

Scope id **`S10-tpd-v1`** means all of the following:
- the trial phase models are {LIQUID: Wilson (strictly convex), VAPOR: ideal gas};
- same-model trials are excluded by §4.2 item 1;
- the vapour-trial distance is in closed form;
- the liquid-trial distance comes from successive substitution to δ_SS = 10⁻¹³ within SS_MAX = 1000 iterations;
- the resolution is ε_tpd = 10⁻¹⁰.

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `STABLE_WITHIN_TESTED_SCOPE` | For a single phase: the other model's minimum distance is ≥ −ε_tpd. For a two-phase result: the two-phase SS converged with 0 < β < 1, which makes it global within the model by §4.2 item 3. |
| `UNSTABLE` | The deciding distance is < −ε_tpd. Only `stability()` returns this; the flash never returns an unstable phase set. |
| `UNKNOWN` | A search reached SS_MAX, the state is dormant, or the state is outside the domain. The result says which (§5.4). |

**On ε_tpd = 10⁻¹⁰.** It is `pr-lib-v1`'s threshold (R-540). It sits 5600 times above the measured binary64 floor of
the distances, 1.78 × 10⁻¹⁴ (claim FLOOR-TM). A state whose deciding distance lies in [−ε_tpd, 0) is kept single-phase:
F7 and F11 are registered because they lie there, and F8 and F10 because they lie just outside. The label then still
reads `STABLE_WITHIN_TESTED_SCOPE`, because the scope includes the resolution.

### 5.1 Construction and records

`WilsonIdealGasProvider(records_path: str | os.PathLike[str] | None = None)`.
- `None` means the real records, `benchmarks/s10/records.yaml`, read from a source checkout. They are not package data
  (§9.1, R-706).
- If that file is absent (before WO-S10-3), construction raises `RecordError("records_missing: …")`. That is an
  explicit refusal, not a fallback.

Records are read at run time, never transcribed into code (the `pr-c1-v1` pattern). The format is
`verification-records.yaml`'s:
- top-level `schema_version: 1`, `record_schema`, `data_set`, `reference_convention`, `synthetic`, `uncertainty`,
  `data_provenance` and `domain`;
- `components`: ComponentRecords, each with the eleven parameters `ideal_gas_cp_b0…b4`, `standard_formation_enthalpy`
  and `vapour_pressure_a…d`;
- `wilson_lambda`: N(N−1) entries.

`load_records` refuses a file with a `RecordError` whose message begins with a code:
- `record_structure`: a missing or unknown key, a duplicate id, or schema_version ≠ 1;
- `record_units`: a parameter whose unit or kind differs from the registration (`"1"`/`dimensionless`;
  `J/mol`/`molar_enthalpy`);
- `record_convention`: the convention is not one of `WILSON-IG-ref-v1` (with `synthetic: false`) or `S10-VER-ref-v1`
  (with `synthetic: true`), or a component's `synthetic` flag differs from the file's;
- `record_lambda`: a missing, duplicate, diagonal, non-finite or non-positive Λ;
- `record_domain`: lo ≥ hi, or T outside the NASA range [200, 1000] K.

It hands over the parsed floats unchanged, bitwise (S10.A02).

### 5.3 `evaluate_phase(request, context)`

**Request checks, in `pr-c1-v1`'s order.** Each is a refusal with empty values, `phase_signature` None, and a message
that begins with the code and a colon:
1. a requested derivative input outside `derivative_order` → `unsupported`, `undeclared_derivative_input: <name>`;
2. a property outside `properties` → `unsupported`, `unknown_property: [<names>]`. This includes `v`, `Z`, `g` and `s`;
3. `len(n) ≠ N` → `error`, `state_length: …`;
4. domain → `out_of_domain`, with `pr-c1-v1`'s message forms: non-finite T or P; T or P outside the box; a flow not
   finite and ≥ 0; a total not finite;
5. dormant (n_tot == 0.0 exactly) → `unsupported`, `dormant_state: composition undefined`.

**Answer.** `status` `ok` and `phase_signature` = the requested phase. `values` holds exactly the requested properties.
`derivatives` holds, for each requested property, exactly the requested inputs. Everything is computed by §3.5–§3.6.
The phase is the one asked about: `evaluate_phase` never decides phases (blueprint §6.3 A01), so a metastable liquid is
answered.

### 5.4 `flash(request, context)`: the TP flash, `S10-tp-flash-v1`

**Step 0, request checks, in `pr-c1-v1`'s order.** Each refusal has no outlets, `vapor_fraction` None and the message
`<code>: …`:
1. a specification other than `"TP"` → `unsupported`, `unsupported_specification: …`;
2. derivatives requested → `unsupported`, `flash_derivatives_unsupported: …`;
3. a wrong length → `error`, `state_length: …`;
4. domain → `out_of_domain`, as in §5.3;
5. dormant → **`ok`**, `ZERO_FLOW`, `vapor_fraction` None, both outlets dormant (zeros at the same T and P), empty
   `k_values`, `iterations` 0, and the message `stability: UNKNOWN; scope: S10-tpd-v1; reason: dormant_state:
   composition undefined (ADR 0001 D3.1, D3.4)`.

**Step 1.** z = n/n_tot, and F = {i : n_i > 0}.

**Step 2, the vapour trial (closed form).** For every i, K_i(z) = exp(ln γ_i(z) + ln P_i^sat(T) − ln P). Then
tm_V = 1 − Σ_{i∈F} z_i K_i(z). **If tm_V ≥ −ε_tpd, the result is LIQUID:**
- `vapor_fraction` 0.0;
- `liquid` = the request's state, its values bitwise; `vapor` dormant at the same T and P;
- `k_values` {} (no K is an answer of a single-phase result);
- `iterations` 0.

**Step 3, the liquid trial.**
- Start from W_i = z_i / K_i(z) for i ∈ F, and W_i = 0 otherwise.
- For m = 1…SS_MAX:
  - set w = W/ΣW and W'_i = z_i / K_i(w) for i ∈ F;
  - set s = max_{i∈F} |ln W'_i − ln W_i|, then W ← W';
  - stop if s ≤ δ_SS.
- Without a stop: `not_converged`, no outlets, and the message `stability: UNKNOWN; scope: S10-tpd-v1; reason:
  stability_not_converged: …`.
- With w = W/ΣW of the final W, evaluate tm_L = 1 + Σ_{i∈F} W_i (ln W_i + ln K_i(w) − ln z_i − 1). This form is
  second-order accurate in W; the shortcut 1 − ΣW is not.
- **If tm_L ≥ −ε_tpd, the result is VAPOR:** `vapor_fraction` 1.0, `vapor` = the request's state bitwise, `liquid`
  dormant, `k_values` {}, and `iterations` m.

**Step 4, two-phase.** K^(0)_i = K_i(w) from step 3's final w, for i ∈ F. For m = 1…SS_MAX:
- **(a) Rachford–Rice.** Require K_max > 1 > K_min over F. Otherwise: `not_converged`, `two_phase_not_converged: no
  Rachford-Rice bracket`. Solve f(β) = Σ_{i∈F} z_i (K_i − 1)/(1 + β(K_i − 1)) = 0 on (1/(1 − K_max), 1/(1 − K_min)) by
  bisection with safeguarded Newton. Stop when the bracket is ≤ 4 ulp(β) wide or f(β) = 0.
- **(b) Compositions.** x_i = z_i/(1 + β(K_i − 1)) and y_i = K_i x_i for i ∈ F; both are 0 otherwise. Normalise
  x ← x/Σx and y ← y/Σy.
- **(c) Update.** K'_i = K_i(x) for i ∈ F, and s = max_{i∈F} |ln K'_i − ln K_i|. If s ≤ δ_K, stop with this (β, x, y)
  and *this* K, the one used in (a). Otherwise K ← K'.

If there is no stop, or the stop has β ∉ (0, 1): `not_converged`, `two_phase_not_converged: …`, label UNKNOWN.

**The two-phase result is `TWO_PHASE`:**
- `vapor_fraction` β;
- `vapor.n_i` = β n_tot y_i and `liquid.n_i` = (1 − β) n_tot x_i, exactly 0.0 for i ∉ F;
- `k_values`: K_i for i ∈ F, and K_i(x) for i ∉ F (infinite dilution in the liquid);
- `iterations`: the step-3 count plus the step-4 count.

**Messages of `ok` results** (R = Python `repr(float)`):

```
LIQUID     stability: STABLE_WITHIN_TESTED_SCOPE; scope: S10-tpd-v1; tm_vapour_trial: R; tm_liquid_trial: not_evaluated
VAPOR      stability: STABLE_WITHIN_TESTED_SCOPE; scope: S10-tpd-v1; tm_vapour_trial: R; tm_liquid_trial: R
TWO_PHASE  stability: STABLE_WITHIN_TESTED_SCOPE; scope: S10-tpd-v1; tm_vapour_trial: R; tm_liquid_trial: R; fugacity_residual: R
```

`fugacity_residual` is step 4's final s.

**Message rule.** Every `ok` or `not_converged` result begins with `stability: `. Every refusal (`unsupported`,
`out_of_domain`, `error`) begins with its code. A result never has neither.

### 5.5 `stability(state, phase, context) -> StabilityReport`

This method is **outside the frozen Protocol**: the Protocol is unchanged, and the method is an addition to the concrete
class (ADR 0047 C1).

```python
StabilityLabel = Literal["STABLE_WITHIN_TESTED_SCOPE", "UNSTABLE", "UNKNOWN"]

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class StabilityReport:
    label: StabilityLabel
    scope: str                                   # "S10-tpd-v1"
    phase: Phase                                 # the phase judged, as given
    tm: Mapping[str, float | None]               # {"VAPOR": tm_V or None, "LIQUID": tm_L or None}: key = trial model
    trial_composition: tuple[float, ...] | None  # normalised trial composition at the minimum
    iterations: int                              # liquid-trial SS iterations (0 when not run)
    residual: float | None                       # last SS step of the liquid trial, else None
    epsilon: float                               # 1e-10
    reason: str                                  # "" when decided; "<code>: <text>" when UNKNOWN
```

**`phase == "LIQUID"`** judges the state as a liquid against vapour trials:
- tm = tm_V by step 2;
- trial composition z∘K(z)/Σ;
- `tm["LIQUID"]` is None.

**`phase == "VAPOR"`** judges it as a vapour against liquid trials:
- tm = tm_L by step 3;
- UNKNOWN on the cap, with reason `stability_not_converged`;
- `tm["VAPOR"]` is None.

**Label:** STABLE_WITHIN_TESTED_SCOPE if tm ≥ −ε_tpd, otherwise UNSTABLE.

The method reports and never raises:
- a wrong length gives UNKNOWN with reason `state_length`;
- out of domain gives UNKNOWN with reason `out_of_domain`;
- dormant gives UNKNOWN with reason `dormant_state`.

### 7.2 Tolerances of `evaluate_phase`

**Rule.** |impl − expected| ≤ TOL_VALUE · max(|expected|, s_q), with TOL_VALUE = 10⁻¹². The scales s_q:

| Quantity | s_q |
| --- | --- |
| h | 10³ J/mol |
| ∂h/∂T | 10 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ |
| ∂h/∂n_j | 10³/n_tot |
| ln φ | 1 |
| ∂ln φ/∂T | 10⁻² K⁻¹ |
| ∂ln φ/∂P | 1/P |
| ∂ln φ/∂n_j | 1/n_tot |

An identity zero (§3.6) is asserted to be exactly `0.0`.

**Argument.**
- **Floor:** a plain binary64 transcription of the closed forms is at most 6.93 × 10⁻¹⁴ scaled from the 50-digit values
  over every E-state value and derivative (worst at E4; claim FLOOR-E requires ≤ 10⁻¹³). The tolerance is therefore 14×
  above it.
- **Catch:** each of the fourteen mutations of §7.4 moves at least one E-assertion by ≥ 10³× its tolerance. The smallest
  is M14 (R = 8.314) at 1.9 × 10⁸×.

### 7.3 Tolerances of the flash and of the trial distances

**Trial distances** (`tm_vapour_trial`, `tm_liquid_trial`, `stability().tm`): |Δ| ≤ TOL_TM = 10⁻¹², absolute.
- Floor: 1.78 × 10⁻¹⁴ (FLOOR-TM), so 56× margin.
- Every classification sits at least 10 TOL_TM from 0 and from −ε_tpd (claims BAND-*, MARGIN-*). An implementation
  within tolerance therefore classifies as expected. F12 is the exception by design: its distance is within roundoff of
  0, and the step order decides it.

**Two-phase outputs** (β, x_i, y_i and both outlets' flows), per state and output, as in the YAML at
`tolerances.flash.per_state`:

τ_q = 10 · ( S_q · A · (δ_K + f_lnK) + 64 ε · max(|q|, s_q) )

- A = ‖(I − ∂Φ/∂ln K)⁻¹‖_∞ is the first-order error amplification of the SS fixed point Φ(ln K) = ln K(T, P, x(RR(K))).
  An SS stopped at step s ≤ δ_K is within A·δ_K of the fixed point in ln K. It is computed at 50 digits; it is 2.23–2.94,
  and 27.4 at F5.
- S_q = Σ_j |∂q/∂ln K_j| is the sensitivity of the Rachford–Rice output to K.
- f_lnK is the measured binary64 floor of ln K at the solution, ≤ 3.55 × 10⁻¹⁵.
- s_q = 1 for fractions and n_tot for flows.

`k_values` are relative: 10 · (A (δ_K + f_lnK) + 64 ε). For an absent component k: 10 · (S_k A (δ_K + f_lnK) + 64 ε).

The resulting tolerances:
- F3, F4, F8, F10: 4.9 × 10⁻¹² to 2.3 × 10⁻¹¹ for β, and 1.9 × 10⁻¹³ to 2 × 10⁻¹¹ for compositions and flows.
- F5: 7.2 × 10⁻⁹ for β and up to 1.3 × 10⁻⁸ mol/s for flows. That is the price of K ≈ 1.

**Catch:** evaluating K at the vapour composition instead of the liquid moves F3's β by 1.6 × 10⁻², which is
1.3 × 10⁹× its tolerance (MUT-F01).

**Single-phase outlets** are asserted bitwise: the feed state in the named phase, and zeros at the same T and P.

**Component balance** of two-phase outlets: |n^V_i + n^L_i − n_i| ≤ 10⁻¹³ n_tot.
- Floor: four roundings, ≈ 4.4 × 10⁻¹⁶ relative, plus the Rachford–Rice residual at 4 ulp.
- Catch: a balance error of 10⁻¹⁰ is 10³× above.

### 8.1 IDAES 2.13 (primary reference), WO-S10-4

**Mapping** (the matched inputs; all recorded in the qualification record):
- `activity_coeff_model="Wilson"`, `state_vars="FTPz"`, `valid_phase=("Vap","Liq")`.
- `vol_mol_comp[i] = 1` for all i, and **`tau[j,i] = −ln Λ_ij`** for i ≠ j. Claim IDAES-MAP: this reproduces our ln γ
  at every E-state at 50 digits. The untransposed `tau[i,j] = −ln Λ_ij` is off by 0.046 at E1, so a transposition is
  caught at the readbacks.

> **Amended by §15 (Amendment 2).** The pressure is 101.3 kPa, as Table II prints it, and methanol + ethanol's 11 points

**Fit datasets** (Q-N1; the specifier's recollection, to be verified on transcription): K. Kurihara, M. Nakamichi and
K. Kojima, "Isobaric vapor–liquid equilibria for methanol + ethanol + water and the three constituent binary systems",
*J. Chem. Eng. Data* 38 (1993) 446–449. Its three binaries at 101.325 kPa are the fit data. Transcription:
- into `benchmarks/s10/vle/kurihara1993_binaries.csv`, each value as printed;
- double-entered by two independent transcriptions and diffed, with the diff empty before commit.

**Substitution rule if the paper cannot be obtained or does not match:** use one isobaric 101.325 kPa dataset per
binary with ≥ 15 points spanning x = 0.05–0.95, from a primary paper. Record it as a build decision, ruled in the review.

**Objective:**
- Φ(θ_ij, θ_ji) = Σ_points Σ_{i ∈ pair, 0.02 ≤ x_i ≤ 0.98} [ln γ_i^exp − ln γ_i(x)]², with
  ln γ_i^exp = ln(y_i P / (x_i P_i^sat(T))) computed with the *fitted* P^sat of §9.3;

- each binary is fitted on its own; Wilson has no ternary parameter.

**Solver:**
- Gauss–Newton at 50 digits from θ = 0, halving the step while Φ increases;
- stop when ‖Δθ‖ < 10⁻³⁰, or fail at 200 iterations;
- then a grid scan of θ ∈ [−3, 6]², step 0.05, must find no Φ below Φ* − 10⁻¹²: a global claim on the box;
- Λ is rounded to 10 significant digits.

**Reported, as fit quality and not validation:** the average absolute deviation (AAD) of the bubble-T and y
back-calculations per binary, and the model's ethanol–water azeotrope against the dataset's own near-azeotrope points.

## 14. Amendment 1: review errata (2026-10-25; R-711)

These come from the `reviewer` pass on WO-S10-1/2 (`docs/reviews/S10-review-wo1-wo2.md`). They change no registered
state, constant, tolerance or reference value.

**E-1. The flowing set is defined on z** (§2 "Flowing", §5.4 step 1, §5.5).
- F = {i : z_i > 0}, where z_i = n_i/n_tot is computed in binary64.
- A component with n_i > 0 whose z_i underflows to 0.0 (n_i < 2⁻¹⁰⁷⁵ n_tot) is treated as absent:
  - a single-phase result still returns the feed bitwise;
  - in a split, its outlet flows are 0.0, an imbalance below 2.5 × 10⁻³²⁴ n_tot.
- **Reason.** Taken with n_i > 0, step 3's ln z_i is ln 0. The search then raises instead of returning a result, and
  `stability()` must never raise.

**E-2. The δ_K floor depends on the state; near an azeotrope it exceeds δ_K** (§5.6, δ_K row; §7.3, the A·δ_K
bound).
- **Where the floor comes from.**
  - The Rachford–Rice β is set by Σ z_i(K_i − 1) against Σ z_i(K_i − 1)².
  - A rounding of u in ln K therefore moves β by about u/|K − 1|², and x by about u/|K − 1|.
  - So the step of step 4(c) has a noise floor of about u‖G‖/min_{i∈F}|K_i − 1|.
- **Where it matters.** The floor exceeds δ_K = 10⁻¹³ once |K_i − 1| ≲ 10⁻⁴ over F, that is, within about 10⁻⁴ in
  composition of an azeotrope and inside the two-phase sliver there.
- **What was measured** (verification records, VB–VC azeotrope, 101 325 Pa):
  - composition offsets of 2 × 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴, five temperatures each inside the sliver (6 × 10⁻⁹ to 1.5 × 10⁻⁷ K
    wide);
  - 7 of the 20 states reach the cap, with the last step between 10⁻¹¹ and 3 × 10⁻¹¹;
  - the rest stop on a noise dip, so their ln K error is the floor, not A·δ_K.
  - Their β carries about 10⁻⁶ relative error. That is the β conditioning itself (∂β/∂T ≈ 10⁸ K⁻¹ there); x and y
    are not affected at that level.
- **Consequences.**
  1. §5.4's contract holds: those states are `not_converged`, with stability UNKNOWN and no outlets, never a wrong
     split.
  2. The §7.3 statement "an SS stopped at s ≤ δ_K is within A·δ_K of the fixed point" applies only where the floor is
     below δ_K. That is the case at F3–F10, so the registered tolerances stand.
  3. §5.6's floor and its "28×" margin are facts of the registered states, not of the method.
- **What follows.**
  - S10 changes no code or constant for this.
  - The build lane adds a near-azeotrope test that only asserts a correct outcome: either `ok` with a valid split or
    `not_converged` with UNKNOWN.
  - The remedy is S10-C's: a stop tied to the conditioning, or an iteration that does not pass x through β. S10-C's
    PH flash searches inside this sliver.

### A2-1. The Λ fit (ESC-1; B-25, B-26 and B-27 ratified)

**What was found.**
1. **Wilson's residual map is rank-deficient on the curve C: θ_12 + θ_21 = 0 (Λ_12 Λ_21 = 1), for every data set.**
   For a binary, with S_1 = x_1 + x_2 Λ_12 and S_2 = x_1 Λ_21 + x_2, eq. 3.8 gives
   - ∂ln γ_1/∂θ_12 = x_2² Λ_12²/S_1² and ∂ln γ_1/∂θ_21 = x_2² Λ_21/S_2²;
   - ∂ln γ_2/∂θ_12 = x_1² Λ_12/S_1² and ∂ln γ_2/∂θ_21 = x_1² Λ_21²/S_2².

   On C, S_2 = Λ_21 S_1, so for both i, ∂ln γ_i/∂θ_21 = Λ_21 ∂ln γ_i/∂θ_12 at every x. The two columns of J are then
   proportional, J = [c, Λ_21 c], and J has rank 1. B-26's identical columns at θ = 0 are the case Λ_21 = 1.

   On C the gradient is ∇Φ = 2 (cᵀr)(1, Λ_21). The derivative along C is dΦ(−a, a)/da = 2 (cᵀr)(Λ_21 − 1). So every
   stationary point of Φ restricted to C, other than a = 0, is a stationary point of Φ.
2. **A nearly ideal pair has its minimum near C.** At θ = 0 both columns equal x_j², so ln γ_i ≈ x_j² (θ_12 + θ_21) to
   first order. Small γ − 1 therefore fixes θ_12 + θ_21 ≈ 0 and leaves the difference to second order.
   - Methanol + ethanol: θ*_12 + θ*_21 = 0.0881.
   - At θ*, JᵀJ has eigenvalues 1.07 × 10⁻³ and 8.69, a condition number of 8.1 × 10³.
3. **Methanol + ethanol's Φ has two local minima**:

   | | θ | Φ | Hessian eigenvalues |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | θ* | (0.4927813450, −0.4047172020) | 4.353489966 × 10⁻⁴ | 2.22 × 10⁻³, 17.38 |
   | on C (a = 0.156377059) | (−0.1563770593, 0.1563770593) | 4.564308503 × 10⁻⁴ | 4.77 × 10⁻⁴, 15.91 |

   - §9.4's start θ = 0 lies on C, and it is drawn to the second minimum.
   - J has rank 1 there, so Gauss–Newton converges sublinearly and reaches the cap. After 200 iterations it is at
     θ = (−0.1633239, 0.1633239), Φ = 4.6126 × 10⁻⁴.
   - The build lane read that second minimum as "not a minimum". It is a strict local minimum, above θ*.
4. **The step-0.05 grid would have certified the wrong minimum.** Its least Φ is 4.601706 × 10⁻⁴, at (−0.15, 0.15),
   which lies above both minima. The step-0.005 grid's least Φ is 4.358783 × 10⁻⁴, at (0.485, −0.400). That is
   5.3 × 10⁻⁷ above Φ* and 2.06 × 10⁻⁵ below the second minimum.

**Ruling.** The build lane's option (a), reduced to a single start: Gauss–Newton runs from the best point of a
step-0.005 grid. θ = 0 is kept as a comparison run only.

**Procedure (normative; it replaces §9.4's "Solver" list).** The objective, the windowing and the parameters are those
of §9.4, unchanged.
1. **Grid.**
   - θ_12 and θ_21 range over G = {m × 0.005 : m = −600, …, 1200}, binary64 products, 1801 values covering [−3, 6].
   - Φ is evaluated in binary64 at all 1801² points (B-25). Each ln γ_i^exp is computed at 50 digits and then rounded.
   - θ_g is the point of least binary64 Φ. A tie goes to the first point in row-major (θ_12, then θ_21) order.
   - Φ_g is Φ(θ_g) re-evaluated at 50 digits.
2. **Gauss–Newton at 50 digits, from θ_g.**
   - **Step:** Δ is the minimum-norm least-squares solution of J Δ = −r. It is taken on the eigenvectors of JᵀJ whose
     eigenvalue exceeds 10⁻⁴⁰ times the largest (B-26).
   - **Halving:** the trial θ + λΔ is tried for λ = 1, ½, ¼, …, and accepted at the first λ with Φ(trial) ≤ Φ(θ). If
     λ falls below 2⁻²⁰⁰, the iterate stays where it is.
   - **Stop:** when the step taken, ‖λΔ‖₂, is below 10⁻³⁰ (B-27). The fit fails at 200 iterations.
   - The result is θ* and Φ*.
3. **Storage:** Λ_ij = exp(−θ*_ij), rounded to 10 significant digits (unchanged).
4. **Comparison run:** the same Gauss–Newton from θ = 0. It is recorded in `fit-report.json` as `start_theta_zero`,
   and it is never adopted.

**The floor, derived.**
1. *The residual has a grid.* Near P = 101 325 Pa ∈ [2¹⁶, 2¹⁷), any computed r_b is a multiple of
   ulp(P) = 2⁻³⁶ = 1.4551915 × 10⁻¹¹ Pa.
2. *T_bub has a coarser one, and it binds.*
   - T_bub is a binary64 variable in [256, 512) K, so ulp(T_bub) = 2⁻⁴⁴ = 5.6843419 × 10⁻¹⁴ K.
   - Between adjacent representable T_bub, r_b moves by the step s_b = |dr_b/dT|·ulp(T_bub).
   - Here dr_b/dT = Σ_i z_i γ_i(z) P_i^sat (d ln P_i^sat/dT), which is 3.71–4.03 × 10³ Pa/K at the 14 I-states.
   - So s_b = **2.11 × 10⁻¹⁰ (I1) to 2.29 × 10⁻¹⁰ Pa (I9)**, which is 14.5–15.7 ulp(P) (`measured.idaes_row_floors`).
   - The best representable T_bub leaves |r_b| up to s_b/2, plus a few ulp(P) of evaluation rounding. Newton steps
     computed in floating point stop within about one step: up to 1.89 × 10⁻¹⁰ ≈ 0.8 s_b in the record, and up to
     2.87 × 10⁻¹⁰ ≈ 1.3 s_b per ESC-4.
3. *The enthalpy rows.* At these states |h| ≤ 2.9 × 10⁵ < 2¹⁹ J/mol, so ulp(h) ≤ 2⁻³⁴ = 5.82 × 10⁻¹¹. The row's h

   attempt passes depends on where the root falls between representable T_bub, not on convergence.

### A3-3. IDAES's dew temperature (ESC-6)

**Fact.** IDAES 2.13.0's `eq_temperature_dew` is
Σ_i z_i P / (γ_i P_i^sat(T_dew)) − 1 = 0, where γ_i is `activity_coeff_comp`.
- `activity_coeff_comp` is the γ of the state block's **liquid** composition (`qualification.json`,
  `inspection.psat_sites_CH3OH_and_dew_equation.eq_temperature_dew`).
- `activity_coeff_comp_dew` (γ at z) is built and never used.
- The bubble equation uses `activity_coeff_comp_bubble`, the γ at z (package source ll. 1828 and 1893, against ll. 2020
  and 2081 for the dew). That is exact, because the bubble liquid is z.

§8's sentence "its dew temperature evaluates γ at the feed composition" is therefore wrong for 2.13.0.

**The corrected reading.** At a state whose liquid composition is x (the smooth flash's liquid at T_eq), IDAES's dew
T_dew^I solves

  F(T_d) := Σ_{z_i>0} z_i P / (γ_i(x) P_i^sat(T_d)) = 1.

- *Two-phase.* x is the flash liquid at T_eq ≈ T.
- *Vapour (I7).* There T_eq = T_dew^I, β = 1, and x is the incipient liquid. So T_dew^I = T_dew exactly.
  - Claim IDAES-DEW-I7: |T_dew^I − T_dew| ≤ 10⁻¹² K; the generator measures 2.1 × 10⁻³⁵ K.
  - With γ at z instead, the result would be off by −1.693 K, so the claim discriminates.
- *Liquid (I6).* There T_eq = T_bub and x = z, so T_dew^I = T_dew^γ(z).

**Theorem: the two-phase region of IDAES's smooth flash is the true one.** At a two-phase state with β ∈ (0, 1) and
x ≠ y, T_dew^I > T.

*Proof.*
1. Equilibrium gives y_i P = x_i γ_i(x) P_i^sat(T), so F(T) = Σ_i z_i x_i/y_i.
2. With z = β y + (1 − β) x, this becomes F(T) = β + (1 − β) Σ_i x_i²/y_i.
3. By Cauchy–Schwarz, (Σ_i x_i)² ≤ Σ_i x_i²/y_i · Σ_i y_i, so Σ_i x_i²/y_i ≥ 1, with equality iff x = y. Hence
   F(T) > 1.
4. Each P_i^sat increases with T, so F decreases strictly, and its root lies above T.
5. As T → T_dew, β → 1 and F(T) → 1, so T_dew^I → T_dew. ∎

So the two implementations have the same phase boundaries. What differs is the distance d_d^I = T_dew^I − T, which sets
the smoothing error of IDAES's smooth minimum.

**Ruling.**
1. **§8's sentence and B-31 are corrected** as above. T_dew^γ(z) is renamed "the γ(z) dew" (YAML `dew_T_gamma_at_z`).
   It is the input of A2-3's rule and nothing else.
2. **The I-states do not move.** A2-3's rule stays the definition of their temperatures. They are registered, the tool
   has run on them, and moving them now would choose states after seeing which ones converge. A2-3's reason ("IDAES's
   smooth flash is two-phase only on [T_bub, T_up]") is withdrawn, and the theorem replaces it.
3. **The generator computes IDAES's dew** at each I-state from our closed-form flash (YAML `idaes_dew_T`): γ at the

### A3-4. Tool-side checks before any comparison (S10.A43), and the envelope reading (S10.A44)

**S10.A43** is computed from tool records only, at the qualification step, before any difference to our values exists.

*(a) Rung dependence.*
- It applies to a state with converged attempts at two or more rungs. Each converged attempt is compared with the
  selected attempt on every A41 quantity:

*(b) Setting dependence.*
- It applies to each (state, rung, linear solver) whose attempt converged both in run R (§8.1's ladder, rerun for
  reproduction) and in run A3 (A3-1's ladder). The two attempts get the same ratio.
- A ratio above 0.1 makes the state `NOT_COMPARABLE(reference_accuracy: setting_dependence(<ratio>))`. The build lane
  then escalates, because A3-1's premise has failed.

*Why 0.1.*
- *It sits above what is expected.*
  - The smoothing shift of T_eq, ε²/(4 d_b) − ε²/(4 d_d^I), has the same sign at every rung and scales as ε². The
    difference between two rungs is therefore smaller than the coarser rung's shift.

  - The solver floor adds about 0.01 τ or less: 10⁻⁹ in mole fraction from `tol`, and ≤ 9.24 × 10⁻¹⁴ from
    `constr_viol_tol`.
  - WO-S10-4 measured |T_eq − T|·|∂q/∂T| ≤ 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ = 0.008 τ at every converged attempt.
  - (b)'s expected ratio is ≤ 0.01: a looser stop moves the iterate by at most one Newton correction.
- *It sits below what is worth catching.*
  - A different root moves β by O(0.1–1), that is 10⁶ τ.
  - An unresolved kink shifts T_eq by about ε/2 = 5 × 10⁻⁵ K at 10⁻⁴. That moves q by ≥ 5 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.0587

*What A43 cannot do.* A state converged at one rung only gets no rung-dependence check; I13 is expected to be one. For

### A4-2. Readings corrected: the A44 text, the A2-1 grid claim, and what the methanol + ethanol fit determines

**1. S10.A44's text is Pyomo's verbose rendering (ESC-7, B-42).** §16 A3-4's "the bubble text" and "the dew text" mean

rendering. The four patterns are unchanged.

**2. LAMBDA-⟨binary⟩ holds by construction under A2-1's procedure.** Gauss–Newton starts at θ_g and accepts a trial only
if Φ(trial) ≤ Φ(θ). So Φ* ≤ Φ(θ_g) = Φ_g identically, and the claim Φ_g ≥ Φ* − 10⁻¹² cannot fail. A2-1's sentence "The
global claim keeps its 10⁻¹² margin, on a grid 100 times denser" is replaced by:

> The grid-global property holds by construction: θ_g is the least binary64 Φ on the grid, the binary64 error near

> LAMBDA-⟨binary⟩ records this and is kept as a guard against a change of start or of the acceptance rule; it is not
> evidence. The evidence that θ* is a minimum is LAMBDA-MIN.

The claim is true, and nothing is loosened by this reading. What A2-1 called a test is a consequence.

**3. Robustness of the single start (measured by the reviewer, not registered).** The procedure was run on 79 variants
of the Table II data: each binary with one point left out (49), and with each printed y_1 moved by −0.001, 0 or +0.001
at random (10 per binary). Every variant meets LAMBDA-CONV, LAMBDA-MIN and LAMBDA-⟨binary⟩. The most iterations were
102 (of 200), at a methanol + ethanol variant whose minimum lies on C. So the procedure is fail-safe. It never adopts a
non-minimum or a point above the grid's best. Its possible failure is a refusal (LAMBDA-CONV, -MIN, -BOX), which needs
an amendment, not a build choice.

The methanol + ethanol *result* is not stable, though. Across the 21 methanol + ethanol variants the adopted minimum lies
in three basins: θ*'s (θ_12 from 0.40 to 0.71), a mirror basin that the committed data do not have (θ_12 ≈ −0.43 to
−0.50, θ_21 ≈ 0.54 to 0.66), and C (one variant). θ_12 + θ_21 stays in [0, 0.175]. This is the objective's near-
degeneracy (A2-1 finding 2), not the solver's: any global method would jump in the same way.

0.46 K, and y by up to 0.009, if the methanol + ethanol Λ are taken at the committed data's second minimum (on C) instead

> **The individual Λ of methanol + ethanol are not determined by Table II.** Only θ_12 + θ_21 is. One printed digit of

**Why bytes differ off x86-64, and what bounds the difference.**
- **The series values.** The rounding error of evaluating S(x̂) = Σ c_k T_k(x̂) on one machine is at most

  A = 4(n + 1)²·u·Σ|c_k|/|P| + |S′(x̂)|·δx̂/|P|, with δx̂ = u(|a + b| + |2T − (a + b)|)/(b − a) + 2u|x̂| + u,

  where x̂ = (2T − (a + b))/(b − a) on the piece [a, b]. The first term is a pessimistic estimate covering the O(n²)
  error growth of either the three-term recurrence or Clenshaw's on [−1, 1]. The second covers the rounding of the
  mapping.
  - The worst A over the 183 points is 3.07 × 10⁻¹³ (CH3OH), 4.14 × 10⁻¹³ (C2H5OH) and 2.31 × 10⁻¹³ (H2O).
  - Two machines therefore differ by at most 2A = 8.27 × 10⁻¹³.
  - The realized error (item 1, at most 17.3 u = 1.9 × 10⁻¹⁵) lies two decades inside A.
- **iapws95-v1's P_σ** (the five cross-check points only). It is a Newton solve to 4ε relative density steps (S05
  §3.5). A value's floor is ε·Σ|terms| ≈ 10⁻¹⁴, and the root adds κ·4ε with κ ≤ 100 away from T_c (S05 §6.2). That is
  ≲ 10⁻¹³ per machine and ≲ 2 × 10⁻¹³ across two.
- **The bound.** B_P = 1e-10 relative is R-627's class-T bound with s_f = 0, the scale S07 gives saturation quantities.
  It was derived as J_max·κ = 1.05 × 10⁻¹² × 100 for Helmholtz-energy quantities through a density root away from T_c
  (CoolProp's jets there). iapws95-v1's P_σ is a computation of that kind, with S05's own floor quoted above. B_P
  bounds the series values with utilization ≤ 0.0083 and the iapws95-v1 values with ≤ 0.002. One bound, R-627's,
  serves both. A tighter class for the series would only detect differences between 10⁻¹² and 10⁻¹⁰, and no mechanism
  worth catching was found there (see what it catches).

**The ruling.**
- **On `platform.machine() == "x86_64"`:** byte equality, unchanged. A difference there means that CoolProp, its
  configuration or the script changed, which is what the check is for (R-627 §20.6's reasoning).
- **Elsewhere:** `classed_problems(committed, recomputed)`:
  - **Exact:** the structure (keys in order, list lengths), every string, integer and boolean, and every float that is
    neither a pressure nor a deviation. The latter are the 183 + 5 temperatures, the grid and the limit, all inputs.
  - **Pressures:** the second element of each `points_T_K_P_Pa` pair, `P_coolprop_Pa` and `P_iapws95_v1_Pa`.
    |Δ| ≤ B_P·|x_committed|, with B_P = 1e-10.
  - **Deviations:** `relative_deviation` and `worst_relative`. |Δ| ≤ B_D = 2.1e-10, absolute.
    - Derivation: dev = p_c/p_i − 1. With p_c and p_i perturbed by relative e₁ and e₂ (|e| ≤ B_P),
      Δdev = (1 + dev)(e₁ − e₂)/(1 + e₂). So |Δdev| ≤ (1 + 10⁻⁸)·2B_P/(1 − B_P) = 2.0000000202 × 10⁻¹⁰.
    - Rounding adds ≤ 2u|dev| ≈ 2 × 10⁻²⁴, since the subtraction is exact (Sterbenz).

### A6-3. The bubble pressure (brief item 3; R-725)

**Procedure.** In `scripts/s10_validation.py`, with the committed records: `d4a39730…`, or a successor in
`records-lineage.json` (whose steps change only the `uncertainty` line, which no computation reads).
1. Read the row: T = float(T_K); x_1 = float(x1); P_exp = 1000.0 × float(P_kPa), in Pa; y_1,exp = float(y1).
2. Embed the binary in the records' order (CH3OH, C2H5OH, H2O):
   - methanol + water: n = (x_1, 0.0, 1.0 − x_1);
   - ethanol + water: n = (0.0, x_1, 1.0 − x_1).
3. Make one call: `evaluate_phase(PropertyRequest(state=StreamState(n=n, temperature=T, pressure=P_EVAL_VAL2),
   phase="LIQUID", properties=("lnphi_CH3OH", "lnphi_C2H5OH", "lnphi_H2O")), None)`, with P_EVAL_VAL2 = 101325.0 Pa.
4. Compute:
   - K_i = exp(lnphi_i);
   - S = n_1 K_1 + n_2 K_2 + n_3 K_3, summed in that order;
   - P_calc = P_EVAL_VAL2 × S;
   - y_i = n_i K_i / S.
   The judged y is the alcohol's (y_CH3OH or y_C2H5OH). Water's y_H2O is reported as a check. The absent alcohol's y is
   exactly 0.

**Why this is the model's bubble pressure, exactly.**
- By (3.10), x_i K_i(T, P) P = x_i γ_i(x) P_i^sat(T) at every P. γ does not depend on P (3.8), and the liquid has no
  Poynting factor (3.3).
- So P_eval Σ x_i K_i(T, P_eval) = Σ x_i γ_i P_i^sat for every P_eval. That is the bubble pressure, and
  Σ x_i K_i(T, P_calc) = 1 holds identically.
- P_eval only has to be a pressure at which the provider answers. Any in-domain value gives the same P_calc to rounding
  (S10.A51.2).
- n sums to 1.0 within one unit in the last place, and the provider normalises it. The difference from using x is at
  the rounding level.

**Why not iterate in P, as VAL-1 iterates in T.**
- VAL-1's bubble T needs a root search because T enters through P^sat(T). Here P is explicit.
- A search would add a stopping rule and a failure mode for no gain in accuracy.
- It would also call the provider at P_calc. For part of the data that lies below the records' domain (A6-1), where the
  provider refuses.

**Refusals and failures.**
- Every registered point has T in [300, 400] K, P_eval in the domain, and finite non-negative flows summing to 1. So
  §5.3's checks admit all 159 states.
- A refusal (status ≠ `ok`) or a non-finite P_calc or y is therefore a defect of the provider or of the script, not a
  validation outcome.
- The script then writes nothing, exits 1, and prints `VAL-2 refused at <system> T=<T_K> row=<row>: <status>:
  <message>` (or `VAL-2 non-finite at <system> T=<T_K> row=<row>`).
- WO-S10-8 escalates it to the design lane as a verification defect. The point is never dropped, imputed, or counted
  as within or outside a band. VAL-2 has no verdict until the defect is resolved.

**Measured floors.** Measured by this amendment on x86-64 at worktree HEAD `7cf51876`, on the synthetic
`verification-records.yaml` only, through the same `evaluate_phase` path.
- States: the binaries (VA, VC), (VB, VC) and (VA, VB), embedded as in step 2; T ∈ {300, 323.15, 333.15, 350, 380,
  420} K; x_1 ∈ {0.02, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 0.98}. That is 126 states.

### A6-4. Aggregation and the verdict (brief items 1 and 2; R-726)

**Per point:** r = (P_calc − P_exp)/P_exp, and Δy_1 = y_1,calc − y_1,exp.

**Per binary b** (methanol + water, N = 52; ethanol + water, N = 107):
- AAD_P(b) = (1/N) Σ |r| and AAD_y(b) = (1/N) Σ |Δy_1|, over the binary's points.
- Both are stored as 10-significant-digit values (VAL-1's `sig`). The verdict is computed from the stored values, so
  it is a function of the report's own numbers.
- b is `within band` if AAD_P(b) ≤ 0.03 and AAD_y(b) ≤ 0.015. The comparisons are inclusive, against §9.6's literals.
  Otherwise b is `not validated within band`.

**VAL-2** is `within band` if both binaries are, and otherwise `not validated within band`. The report and the verdict
name each binary and each metric that missed.

**Reported, never judged:**
- per binary: the maximum |r| and |Δy_1|; the signed means of r and Δy_1; the mean |Δy_H2O| (y_H2O,calc against
  1 − y_1,exp, equal to AAD_y within rounding); and the counts of points whose measured or computed pressure lies below
  the records' lower P bound;
- per (binary, T) isotherm: N, AAD_P, AAD_y and the signed means;
- pooled over the 159 points: AAD_P and AAD_y.

**Why the binary.**
- The parameters are per binary: each pair has its own Λ_12 and Λ_21 (§9.4). §9.6 says that VAL-2 "tests the
  constant-Λ extrapolation in T", which is a statement about each pair's Λ. So each pair is judged on its own points.
- Requiring both binaries follows VAL-3, which judges each component, and VAL-1, which judges each measured y. A miss
  of any judged unit is a miss.
- *Rejected: pooled over 159 points.* Ethanol + water has 107 of the 159, so a pooled mean is two-thirds one binary. A
  methanol + water miss could pass pooled. The pooled means are reported.
- *Rejected: per (binary, T) block, each to pass.* That is six units of 14 to 37 points, judged on twelve statistics.
  The blocks are the paper's measurement runs, 5 K apart, not a distinction the model makes. Twelve tests raise the
  chance that one block crosses a band by scatter while its binary does not. The blocks' signed means are reported, and
  a trend in T would show there.
- *Rejected: a maximum-deviation band as well.* §9.6's VAL-2 has none, and adding one moves the band (R-708).

**Why the measured denominator.**
- P_exp is the reference the model is judged against, and it does not depend on the model. With P_calc as the
  denominator, the metric's scale moves with the thing judged. An over-prediction by a factor of 2 would read as 50 %,
  an under-prediction by the same factor as 100 %.
- With P_exp, |r| is the relative error a user of the model meets at a measured state.
- *Rejected: P_calc,* for the reason above. *Rejected: |ln(P_calc/P_exp)|.* It is a fine metric, but it is not what
  "ΔP/P" says. At 3 % the two differ by about 1.5 % of the value, and the band was written as ΔP/P.

**Why y_1.**
- The paper prints y_1 only. So y_2 = 1 − y_1 carries no measurement of its own, and the model's y_H2O equals
  1 − y_1,calc to rounding.
- So |Δy_2| = |Δy_1|, and judging both would count one error twice. This is B-73's argument, ruling 2 of the VAL-1
  verdict. For a binary, "AAD(y_i)" is AAD(y_1).

### A6-5. Which points enter (brief item 4; R-726)

**All 159 points enter. There is no exclusion rule.**
- *No outlier rule.* A rule chosen now could rest only on the stated accuracies, and those are far inside the bands
  (A6-1). A rule chosen after the computation would be a fit to the validation data.
- *Points below the records' P domain enter.*
  - The computation calls the provider at P_eval only (A6-3), and the model's pressure dependence is (3.10)'s at every
    P.
  - The domain's lower bound is the records' declared envelope for state queries (R-710). It is not a limit of the
    closed forms.
  - Excluding these points would drop the water-rich end of every isotherm and nearly all of ethanol + water at
    323.15 K. That is a selection by composition and temperature, which is the extrapolation VAL-2 exists to test.
  - They are counted in the report and in the `uncertainty` sentence, and the domain is not extended (A6-13).
- *The non-monotone pressures near the ethanol–water azeotrope* enter as printed. The image-only entry reproduced them.
- *Rejected: judging only the points inside the domain,* because it selects. *Rejected: a subset AAD reported beside
  the judged one,* because it invites citing whichever is better.

**The transcription is fixed.**
- After this amendment the committed file changes only by a design-lane amendment. An amendment made after the
  computation must report the verdicts both before and after the change.
- A suspected transcription error found once the numbers are known goes to the design lane with the page-image
  evidence. The build lane does not correct it.
